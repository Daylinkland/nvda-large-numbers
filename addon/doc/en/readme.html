Large Number Reader

Speaks long numbers as words instead of one digit at a time, in English or Spanish.

What it does

  • 1500000 is spoken as "one million five hundred thousand", or in Spanish "un millón quinientos mil".
  • 1235892 is spoken as "one million two hundred thirty five thousand eight hundred ninety two", or "un millón doscientos treinta y cinco mil ochocientos noventa y dos".
  • 1000000000000000 is spoken as "one quadrillion", or in Spanish "mil billones".
  • Numbers from one million up to 36 digits are handled.
  • Numbers shorter than seven digits are not touched, so 500000 and ordinary small numbers are read exactly as before.

Languages

English and Spanish are built in. By default the number language follows NVDA's own language, so a Spanish NVDA speaks Spanish numbers and an English NVDA speaks English numbers without any setting.

To force a language, open NVDA menu, Preferences, Settings, Large Number Reader, and choose the number language. "Automatic" follows NVDA. Spanish uses the long scale, so billón means a million millions (10^12), and a thousand millions is "mil millones".

Commands

KeyAction
NVDA+8Turns large number reading on or off. The setting is remembered next time NVDA starts.

The key can be changed in NVDA menu, Preferences, Input Gestures, Large Number Reader.

What it leaves alone

To avoid breaking other text, the add-on does not rewrite:

  • numbers shorter than seven digits
  • numbers with a leading zero, such as 01234567 (usually an ID)
  • numbers glued to letters, such as 0x1234567 or abc123456
  • dated or version style text such as 2026.2 or 192.168.1.100
  • phone number style pairs such as 555-1234567
  • numbers right after a currency sign, such as $1234567
  • text spoken while spelling or reading character by character

Spoken forms and the word lists

The words come from small text files inside the add-on: data/number_words.txt and data/scale_words.txt for English, and data/number_words_es.txt and data/scale_words_es.txt for Spanish. Each group of three digits reuses the same 0 to 999 table, which keeps the whole add-on well under a tenth of a megabyte. If a file is missing or damaged the add-on builds the words itself, so speech never breaks.

Limits

  • Numbers longer than 36 digits are read in blocks of three digits, still not as one long digit string, but without scale names.
  • The add-on changes what is spoken, not what is displayed, so braille output still shows the original digits.
